Phase 04 ✓ Complete (Issue Found)
Wait-Time Regression: Feature Leakage Discovery
Transitioned to continuous XGBoost Regressor for wait-time prediction. Initial results: MAE 5.25 ts, R² 0.833. Critical issue identified: feature smaller_jobs_in_queue contributed 82% of importance — essentially encoding FIFO queue rank directly. At inference (proactive reordering), this feature becomes meaningless or misleading.
XGBoost Regressor train_wait_model.py Feature leakage detected ⚠ Action required
Critical Issue: Data leakage via dominant feature encoding queue position. In FIFO scheduler, this feature reflects job arrival order. When queue is reordered (Phase 05+), the feature becomes invalid for new job arrivals — creates harmful model bias at inference time.
Phase 05 ✓ Complete (Fix Implemented)
Feature Leakage Fix & Model v2 Retraining
Replaced leaky features with 6 principled cluster-state signals valid at any scheduling policy: (1) can_fit_now, (2) gpu_fit_ratio, (3) fragmentation, (4) queue_pressure, (5) node_availability, (6) avg_free_per_node. Retrained on clean 2,200-sample dataset with 12 total features. No single feature dominates; top 4 contribute 15–23% each. Model now learns genuine cluster dynamics.

What the fix actually bought: removing the leaky smaller_jobs_in_queue feature did not inflate accuracy — it removed a fake signal. The clean v2 model scores R² 0.837 / MAE 4.69 ts on a proper 20% holdout (5-fold CV MAE 4.74 ± 0.42). An earlier headline of R² ≈ 0.96 was an in-sample artifact of a split-after-fit bug and was retired as dishonest in the July 2026 audit. The value of v2 is a model that learns genuine cluster state and stays valid once the queue is reordered, which the leaky model could not.

Phase 13 ✓ Complete
Fairness & Starvation Prevention Analysis
Implemented comprehensive fairness metrics: maximum wait time, Gini coefficient (inequality), completion-time analysis by job size, starvation count (jobs waiting >threshold). The anti-starvation bumping variant recovers some tail (max-wait 87.2 ts, Gini 0.688) but raises starved-job count (18.2 → 30.2) and lowers SLA compliance (0.933 FIFO / 0.945 proactive / 0.863 anti-starvation). Phase 25 ✓ Complete (Closed in v3.2)
Real-Trace Validation on Two Production Workloads
Downloaded and evaluated two real Parallel Workloads Archive traces (LANL-CM5-1994-4.1-cln, SDSC-SP2-1998-4.2-cln, committed under 02_data/). Method: chronological replay of each recorded schedule reconstructs 8 honestly-derivable cluster-state features at every arrival; the synthetic model is tested zero-shot via a rescaled 12-feature mapping with best-case affine calibration, and retrained models use a chronological 80/20 split on log1p(wait minutes). LANL CM-5 (1024 procs, 1994–96, 122,055 usable jobs; median wait ≈ 4 s — largely interactive): transfer R² ≈ −0.001 (MAE 41 min); retrained R²(log) 0.101, MAE 33.7 min vs median-baseline R² −0.106 / 33.5 min — a weak-signal machine, an honest partial negative. SDSC SP2 (128 nodes, 1998, 42,117 evaluated arrivals; heavy-tailed batch queue, p90 wait ≈ 15 h): transfer R² 0.072 (MAE 452 min); retrained R²(log) 0.494, MAE 622 min vs median baseline R² −0.694 / MAE 684 min and rolling-mean R² 0.016 / MAE 1009 min.
LANL-CM5-1994-4.1-cln SDSC-SP2-1998-4.2-cln real_trace_validation.py build_real_trace_datasets.py
Real-trace R² — zero-shot transfer vs retrained vs median baseline
Strong result on the batch machine: once retrained, honest cluster-state features explain roughly half the log-wait variance on a real batch supercomputer (SDSC SP2, R²(log) 0.494) — far ahead of the median (−0.694) and rolling-mean (0.016) baselines.
Honest limits: zero-shot sim-to-real transfer is ≈ 0 on both traces — now quantified on real data, not just the proxy. And signal strength is machine-dependent: on the largely interactive LANL CM-5, even the retrained model only reaches R²(log) 0.101 (MAE 33.7 min vs 33.5 min for the median baseline) — a partial negative. "Retrain per deployment" is now evidence-backed rather than a caveat.

v3.2 Extensions ✓ Complete · July 2026
v3.2 Extensions — Real Traces, Backfill, Fairness Budget, Uncertainty
Four research extensions, all regenerated by the seeded pipeline. (1) Real traces: Phase 25 closed — see the phase entry above. (2) Seven-scheduler benchmark (20 paired runs, out-of-training seeds 1000+i): PROACTIVE beats FIFO by 6.5% on fresh seeds (vs 7.7% near-training — good generalization). Semantics matter: this benchmark's "FIFO" dispatches every fitting job each tick, i.e. unrestricted no-reservation backfilling; EASY backfill adds a hard reservation for the head job (never delayed; perfect runtime estimates make it a strongest-baseline). The reservation costs ≈ 45% mean wait but delivers the best fairness in the whole study (Gini 0.365, max wait ≈ 55 ts). A hybrid that backfills in predicted-wait order ties EASY — an honest null result. Utilisation (0.637) and throughput are identical across all seven schedulers. (3) Bounded-fairness wait budget (20 paired runs, seeds 5000+i, FIFO reference mean 22.4 ts): jobs exceeding a wait budget B escalate to the front, tracing a smooth, tunable Pareto frontier between FIFO and pure proactive. (4) Uncertainty-aware scheduling (quantile XGBoost q10/q50/q90; 10 paired runs per scenario, seeds 7000+i): q50 MAE 4.61 vs 4.69 for the point model; [q10,q90] empirical coverage 68% vs 80% nominal — under-dispersed, reported honestly. The GUARDED policy's spread trigger τ is set at the p95 of in-distribution relative spread.

A tunable fairness dial: B=60 keeps over half the mean-wait gain (+7.1% of the unbounded +13.2%) while capping max wait at 81 ts instead of 136. Operators can pick their point on the frontier rather than choosing between FIFO and pure proactive.
Reported honestly: tiny budgets (B = 0–30) are slightly worse than FIFO (−0.4% to −1.7%) — constant escalation churn without reordering freedom. And in the uncertainty study, UCB(q90) and spread-guarded policies match PROACTIVE in-distribution (+6.1–6.3% vs +6.5%) and under overload (all +38.7–39.0%, with the caveat that this is among-started-jobs under horizon censoring and the smart policies also started more jobs, 131 vs 114) — but on a light-load small cluster (0.5× arrivals, 4 nodes) all smart policies go negative (−4.3% to −5.0% vs FIFO) while the spread guard fires on only 0.2% of ticks. The under-dispersed intervals fail to detect that regime: interval width is not a reliable OOD alarm here, and the drift-triggered FIFO fallback (rolling-MAE monitor, Phase 18) remains the deployment mechanism.
Key Findings & Insights
Feature Leakage, Honestly Handled: Phase 04 identified data leakage (smaller_jobs_in_queue at 82% importance). Phase 05 replaced it with 12 principled cluster-state signals. This did not inflate accuracy — it removed a fake signal. The clean model scores R² 0.837 / MAE 4.69 ts on a 20% holdout (5-fold CV MAE 4.74 ± 0.42; tuned 4.57 / 0.850). A previously reported R² ≈ 0.96 was an in-sample artifact of a split-after-fit bug and was retired as dishonest in the July 2026 audit. The value is a model that stays valid once the queue is reordered.
Statistical Significance: the 40-run paired benchmark (Phase 09) confirms a 7.71% mean wait-time reduction (± 8.87%) with paired t-test p = 1.4e-06 and Wilcoxon p = 7.1e-06; bootstrap 95% CI [4.95%, 10.34%], 33/40 runs positive. The effect on the mean is small but real and reproducible.
Fairness Trade-off (not preserved): the mean-wait gain comes at a cost. Versus FIFO, plain proactive reordering more than doubles tail latency (mean max-wait 57.9 → 124.8 ts) and worsens the Gini coefficient of waits (0.526 → 0.796). GPU utilisation is unchanged (≈64%). The anti-starvation variant trims the tail (87.2 ts, Gini 0.688) but raises the starved-job count (18.2 → 30.2) and lowers SLA compliance to 0.863; the tail-latency cost must be weighed against the mean improvement.
Real-trace validation (Phase 25 closed in v3.2): on two real Parallel Workloads Archive traces, zero-shot sim-to-real transfer is ≈ 0 (LANL CM-5 R² ≈ −0.001, SDSC SP2 R² 0.072 even with best-case affine calibration) — but retraining on trace history recovers strong signal where queueing dominates: SDSC SP2 R²(log) 0.494 vs −0.694 for the median baseline. Signal strength is machine-dependent — the largely interactive LANL CM-5 yields only R²(log) 0.101, an honest partial negative. "Retrain per deployment" (with the Phase 11 OOD result: mean R² −0.31 across 72 shifted scenarios) is now evidence-backed, not a caveat.
The reservation price, and a dial to pay it gradually: EASY backfill's hard reservation for the head job costs ≈ 45% mean wait (24.94 vs 17.22 ts for no-reservation FIFO dispatch) but buys the best fairness in the whole study (Gini 0.365, max wait ≈ 55 ts); a hybrid using predicted-wait backfill order ties EASY — a null result, reported as such. The bounded wait budget spans the space in between: B=60 keeps over half the proactive gain (+7.1%) at max wait 81 ts, while tiny budgets (0–30) are slightly worse than FIFO.
Uncertainty-aware scheduling — parity, not protection: quantile models (q50 MAE 4.61) match the point model in-distribution (+6.1–6.3% vs +6.5%) at no cost, but the [q10,q90] intervals are under-dispersed (68% coverage vs 80% nominal) and the spread guard fires on only 0.2% of ticks in the light-load regime where all smart policies go negative (−4.3% to −5.0%). Interval width is not a reliable OOD alarm here; the drift-triggered FIFO fallback remains the deployment mechanism.
Complete & Reproducible Study: the 30-phase pipeline includes OOD sensitivity analysis, online learning, concept drift detection, scaling studies, real-trace evaluation on two Parallel Workloads Archive machines, and a one-command reproducibility kit (12/12 steps green after the July 2026 audit, which fixed 37 verified defects). The fairness trade-offs, the zero-shot transfer failure, and the machine-dependence of the real-trace signal above mark the honest boundaries of what can be claimed.
